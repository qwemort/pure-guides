How to avoid GTA 6 spoilers on YouTube, Reddit and X

Updated 4 November 2026

Grand Theft Auto VI is scheduled for November 19, 2026 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. As of this writing no PC version has been announced (source). That means a long stretch where console players finish the story, post about it, and everyone else is still waiting. Here is where spoilers actually come from, what you can switch off, and what no setting will catch.

Disclosure: I build a browser extension for this, Pure Spoiler Shield. It is described near the end, after the steps that need no extension. Everything above that section works without installing anything.
Short version
  1. Mute the obvious words on X, and mute spoiler-heavy communities on Reddit.
  2. On YouTube, tell it "Don't recommend channel" for channels that cover the game, and do not open the comments.
  3. Do not type the game's name into search boxes. Autocomplete and "related searches" are a common leak.
  4. Tell friends and group chats to put spoilers behind a warning, or mute those chats for a while.
  5. Accept that manual filters miss things. Plan to step away from the game's tags for the first weeks.

Where the spoilers come from

  • Video titles and thumbnails. A character's face, a location or the words "ending explained" are often enough. This is the hardest one, because the spoiler is in the thing that asks you to click.
  • Comments. On any video or post about the game, the top comments are usually the people who have already finished it.
  • Search suggestions. Typing the game's name can offer completions such as "ending", "death" or a character name.
  • Recommendations and Shorts. One click on a clip about the game and the feed fills with more of it.
  • Trends and replies on X. Trending topics and the first replies under any post that mentions the game.
  • Reddit front pages. A post title is public even if you never open the thread. Spoiler tags are optional and many people skip them.
  • People. Group chats, Discord servers and streamers you already follow.

X: mute the words

X has a built-in Muted words list under Settings and privacy, Privacy and safety, Mute and block. Posts containing a muted word or phrase are hidden from your timeline and notifications, and you can choose how long the mute lasts. Start with the phrases people use when they spoil: the game's name plus words like ending, final mission, dies, spoiler, and the names of main characters you do not want to see. Keep the list short and specific, because muting the game's name alone also hides the harmless trailer talk you may want.

YouTube: shape the feed, skip the comments

YouTube has no keyword mute. What it does give you:

  • In the three-dot menu on a video, Not interested and Don't recommend channel reduce how much of that kind of content you are shown.
  • Turn off autoplay, so the next video is not chosen for you.
  • Do not scroll Shorts while the game is fresh. A single Short can contain the ending, and you will not see the title first.
  • Skip the comments on any video about the game, including trailers.

Reddit: mute the communities

You can mute a community from its menu so its posts stop appearing in your home feed. Muting the main game community and the ones around it removes most of the spoiler titles that would otherwise reach you. Be aware that r/popular and search results can still show the game, and that a thread title alone can be the spoiler. The safest rule is to avoid opening any thread about the game until you finish it.

Google and other search boxes

  • Avoid searching the game's name. If you need something specific, search for the practical thing (a mission, a map feature) rather than the game plus a story word.
  • News and "Top stories" blocks at the top of results are written for the widest audience, so they tend to be careful. Forum and video results below them are not.

People and chats

Most late-night spoilers come from friends. Say it once, early: "I have not finished it, please use a spoiler warning." Mute busy group chats and Discord servers for the first weeks, and unfollow streamers or channels that will cover the story, until you are done.

What no manual setting can catch

  • Words you did not think of. A mute list only knows what you typed. Character and place names that appear only in the finished game are impossible to list in advance.
  • Images and video frames. A screenshot or thumbnail can spoil something that no text filter will ever see.
  • Sites without filters. Many forums, news sites and apps have no mute option at all.

So the realistic plan is layered: mute what you can, keep out of the game's communities, and treat everything else as a risk until you have played.

How to choose any spoiler blocker

  1. Permissions. Which sites can it read? Prefer one that works on the sites you need and asks for more only when you ask.
  2. Local or cloud. Does your browsing leave the device? It should not need to.
  3. Account. Do you need to sign in to filter your own feed?
  4. Honest limits. A good tool says what it cannot catch.
  5. Escape hatch. Can you reveal a hidden item deliberately and turn it off for a site?
